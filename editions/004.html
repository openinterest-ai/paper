A judge acquits the brothers in Ethereum's $25 million trading case.

Judge Jessica Clarke entered judgments of acquittal for Anton and James Peraire-Bueno on November 16, 2026. [1] She found that prosecutors failed to prove a material misrepresentation, an essential element of their wire-fraud case. [2]

The ruling clears both defendants of wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering. [1] Their trial had ended with a deadlocked jury; Clarke then considered whether the government's evidence could legally sustain convictions. [2]

The case concerned an April 23, 2023 operation called Omakase, which targeted automated traders on Ethereum. [3] The court describes bait transactions that attracted bots seeking profits by placing trades around other users' transactions. [3] The brothers exploited a software vulnerability to inspect private transactions, then substituted trades that defeated the bots' strategy. [4]

The opinion puts the resulting gain at $25 million in twelve seconds, the operation's execution time. [2] But Clarke rejected the government's proposed falsehoods, including theories about validator registration, block queries and the submission of a modified block. [5] Frustrating MEV-Boost's intended design did not itself establish the misrepresentation required for fraud, she wrote. [5]

Prosecutors also pointed to concealment and evidence that the brothers knew their conduct was wrong. [6] Clarke held that fraudulent intent could not replace proof of a material falsehood. [6] The laundering conspiracy failed because its alleged criminal proceeds depended on the wire-fraud charge. [1]

Clarke expressly separated this decision from whether the conduct was moral or violated crimes prosecutors had not charged. [2] She noted serious due-process concerns but did not decide that constitutional question. [7]

Optimizely removes malicious code from customer projects. [8]

Optimizely says it fixed a vulnerability that let an unauthorized party insert JavaScript into customer projects. [8] Report URI's Scott Helme found infected code in 188 of 1,611 examined scripts, covering 178 organizations, including Ledger. [9] He reports that the code replaced cryptocurrency addresses on pages and clipboards with attacker-controlled destinations. [9]

His November 17, 2026 update says all infected files his team found were clean by November 16, 2026, 23:11 UTC. [9]

The CFTC suspends Kalshi's Trump speech contracts. [10]

The CFTC stayed the listing of Kalshi's Trump nickname market and TRUMPSAY series while examining their compliance certification. [10] Its order says Kalshi has not adequately demonstrated that these contracts resist manipulation. [10] Kalshi must answer by December 11, 2026; the order makes no final finding that the contracts violate federal law. [10]

CEO Tarek Mansour disputes the decision, arguing that presidential remarks have economic significance, according to Cointelegraph's November 17, 2026 report. [11]

The World Bank brings Circle and Citi into its payments exploration. [12]

The World Bank Group announced the collaboration on November 17, 2026, to examine stablecoins alongside its existing cross-border disbursement arrangements. [12] Citi would connect banking infrastructure, while Circle would supply infrastructure for issuing and settling USDC. [12] The initial exploration concerns an intermediated model in borrower countries that permit digital assets; the announcement supplies no measured savings. [12]

Kiraly's simulations expose the bandwidth cost of proof aggregation. [13]

Csaba Kiraly's November 18, 2026 study models EIP-8288, which would combine transaction-verification work into shared cryptographic proofs. [13] At fifteen signature transactions per second, pushing proofs to six peers consumed 11.9–13.3 megabits per second per node in the tested networks. [13] Kiraly argues that specifications must define who aggregates, when peers fetch proofs and which prover hardware sets the interval. [13]

These are simulator results across proposed designs, not measurements of a deployed Ethereum upgrade. [13]

tech

Santos tests alternative quantum-resistant signatures inside Ethereum proofs. [14]

Manuel B. Santos published experimental leanVM measurements for five signature schemes on November 16, 2026. [14] Raccoon-G verification exceeded the implementation's witness limit, requiring separate proofs that the experiment then combined. [14] Santos sketches wallets retaining signing keys while external aggregators perform proving, but notes that EIP-8288's generic proof path remains incomplete. [14]

Neuder challenges Ethereum's proposed quantum deadline. [15]

Mike Neuder's November 15, 2026 essay argues that rushing quantum resistance could undermine promises already made through Ethereum's upgrade process. [15] Ethlabs proposes readiness in 2027 and says other features must be reconsidered, including FOCIL, a proposed censorship-resistance mechanism. [16] Neuder favors hardening existing software immediately and targeting quantum resistance in 2028; he says dropping FOCIL would damage governance credibility. [15]

White disputes the crypto lobby's election victory narrative. [17]

Molly White's November 13, 2026 analysis challenges Fairshake's advertised success by separating competitive races from safer bets. [17] Her tally gives Fairshake victories in all 74 races with clear or likely favorites, against ten of eighteen competitive races across election rounds. [17] White reports that Fairshake nevertheless claimed a meaningful role in competitive victories; her methodology uses reported spending and race ratings, rather than proving electoral causation.
