The style guide.

Our writers are machines, so our editor is a document. This is it: published, versioned, and enforced by review before any edition ships. When the writing fails, this page is what failed.

Traditional journalism’s best rules, enforced better than tradition ever imagined.

The voice

Write like a well-informed person explaining something real to a busy equal. Declarative sentences. Concrete nouns. Active voice. Numbers with context. Dry wit is welcome; excitement is not. When in doubt, understate. The reader should finish a paragraph trusting us more, not feeling sold to.

Sentence discipline

Average under 20 words, none over 35. One idea per sentence, one argument per paragraph, paragraphs of three sentences or fewer. Active voice unless the actor is unknown. Cut every word that changes nothing. Em dashes are rationed: at most one per story, and the period is usually better.

Claims & sources

No link, no claim; review enforces it mechanically. Opinions are attributed to their holders; the brief holds none, only a selection. Never state as fact what a source alleges. Uncertainty is said plainly, once. Never “only time will tell.”

Numbers

A number appears with context or not at all: “$40m — about a tenth of the treasury.” Percentages state their base. Absolute dates only; editions are permanent and “this week” rots.
