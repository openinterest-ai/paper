The Justice Department seeks forfeiture of more than $1.1 billion in frozen USDC.

Federal prosecutors filed a civil claim in Washington on 23 November 2026 for tokens they trace to the Binance theft. Circle told prosecutors it would issue replacement USDC for the government if the court orders forfeiture. [1] [2]

The complaint in case 1:26-cv-04150 lists 36 address entries across 18 blockchains, counting the same address separately on each network. Prosecutors say the tokens trace to the 29 October 2026 theft of about $6 billion in cryptocurrency. Investigators attribute the theft to the North Korean group they call Lazarus or APT38. [2]

One Ethereum transfer shows the scale of the case. A transaction on 29 October 2026 moved 780 million USDC to the wallet the complaint calls Address A. The chain records the transfer, and prosecutors allege it was unauthorised. [3] [2]

Circle, which issues the dollar token, blocked access to the USDC at the addresses, according to the complaint. It told prosecutors it could freeze the tokens in place but lacked the technical means to transfer them. Its proposed substitute would give the United States an equal quantity of newly issued USDC. [2]

The new case follows a 12 November 2026 order that temporarily restrained a wider set of assets. Prosecutors asked the court to keep the freeze in place during the forfeiture case. Their motion says Binance can secure the release of one address outside the new claim once the earlier order expires. [4]

The complaint asks the court to award the funds to the United States. The Justice Department says the action is part of its effort to help Binance recover losses. How any proceeds would reach Binance remains open in the filings. [2] [1]

OKXICE opens round-the-clock securities trading to eligible U.S. customers.

OKX says the venue opened on 23 November 2026 and will introduce 113 stocks and other exchange-traded products in stages. It says customers hold tokens backed by underlying shares in their own wallets, while the venue restricts which wallets can trade. Grayscale lists a leveraged Zcash fund on NYSE Arca.

Grayscale says its ZECX fund began trading on 23 November 2026. [7] [8] The fund targets twice the daily total return of Grayscale's existing Zcash fund, before fees and expenses. It uses swaps linked to that fund and resets its leverage daily. [8] [9]

StraitsX and OCBC announce a stablecoin payments trial for businesses.

StraitsX announced the pilot on 23 November 2026 and named NTT Docomo Asia as its first participant. StraitsX will match incoming stablecoin payments to specific businesses. OCBC will provide the conversion to conventional currency and credit participating clients' existing bank accounts. [10]

Kev Wedderburn outlines Ethereum's planned switch to hash-based signatures.

On 23 November 2026, Foundation researcher Kev Wedderburn set out plans for validators and wallets to adopt hash-based signatures. He also proposed hash-based commitments for the data bundles Ethereum calls blobs. [11] A draft co-authored by Justin Drake would let validators register new public keys before a separate upgrade starts using them. [12]

NEAR details how its swap service screens transfers.

On 19 November 2026, NEAR described how its Intents swap service screens funds before accepting them. The screening runs in the swap service, leaving transfers on the base network outside its remit. NEAR cited co-founder Illia Polosukhin's argument that decentralised networks can still coordinate their defences. [13] NEAR says it blocked about 96% of the Binance theft-linked flows that reached Intents by 3 November 2026. [13]

culture

Sky borrowers and depositors dispute the terms of a rescue.

Sky's stUSDS pool lends depositors' USDS against staked SKY and needs spare cash to pay withdrawals. [14] On 23 November 2026, co-founder Rune Christensen said he had repaid 3 million USDS, still owed about 77 million and had contributed to depositors' inability to withdraw. [15] Forum participant misher disclosed a loan and proposed a 10% annual borrowing rate and a halt to new loans. Another participant, ares, called for mandatory repayments and either a withdrawal queue or proportional payouts. [16]

culture

Omid Malekan challenges the case for handpicked validators.

In a post dated 22 November 2026, Malekan argued that companies controlling who validates a blockchain can use that power to restrict access. He questioned whether a chain can promise open use while choosing who confirms transactions. [17] Matt Huang laid out Tempo's approach on 6 September 2025. He said anyone would be able to transact from launch, while only approved validators would confirm transactions at first. [18]
