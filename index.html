Chainalysis begins restoring compliance tools after exposing users' credentials.

The company's 6 November 2026 update announces a phased reopening of transaction monitoring and address screening. Its investigation tools remain under repair. [1]

On 30 October 2026, Chainalysis said it had found no evidence of compromised credentials or session tokens. Its 4 November 2026 disclosure reversed that assessment. Names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users had been exposed, the company said. [1]

The affected monitoring service sits inside customers' compliance operations. Chainalysis describes KYT as assessing incoming and outgoing cryptocurrency transfers, tracing sources and destinations associated with illicit activity. Its advertised purpose includes helping customers decide when to freeze funds and integrating transaction checks into their compliance programs. [2]

The product also monitors exposure to sanctioned entities and lets teams investigate alerts through Reactor. Chainalysis describes that connection as a route from transaction screening into more detailed investigations. [2]

Chainalysis says abnormal activity prompted it to shut down KYT, address screening and the login page on 4 November 2026. It notified customers whose KYT accounts appeared to have suffered unauthorized access. The company said it had no evidence of accessed investigation data or manipulated attribution data. [1]

Restored access requires trusted IP addresses, password resets and replacement API keys where applicable. The 6 November 2026 notice gives no completion date for restoring Reactor and Data Solutions. Its reopening announcement therefore establishes partial recovery, not a clean bill of health for every product. [1]

Zcash's NU7 retires the Sprout spending route. [7]

Zcash reached NU7's specified activation height, block 3,543,405, on 6 November 2026 at 02:34 UTC. [7] [8] The upgrade cuts target block spacing from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, with rewards adjusted to preserve daily issuance. [9] It also rejects version 4 transactions, leaving funds in the original Sprout privacy pool unspendable because that pool has no replacement transaction format. [7]

tech

A proof engine reports faster proving with unchanged proof bytes. [10]

Researcher jay-oraclizer introduced StateSync-GKR's results on 7 November 2026, including machine-checked components of its mathematical security argument. [10] The author reports release 1.2 proving 2.41 to 2.71 times as fast as release 1.1 in specified internal benchmarks, with identical proof bytes. [10] The analysis excludes the transformation used to make the running protocol non-interactive, so the formal results do not certify the complete implementation. [10]

Jason Somensatto's 6 November 2026 response welcomes Treasury and the Federal Reserve abandoning a proposed expansion of transfer reporting. [11] He argues that regulators must count the privacy costs borne by people whose financial information institutions collect, alongside firms' compliance costs. Molly White challenges Fairshake's political intimidation story. [12]

White's 4 November 2026 assessment says Sherrod Brown won Ohio's Senate race despite more than $64 million in crypto spending against him and for Jon Husted. [12] She puts that expenditure at nearly five times the industry's spending on any other race in the election cycle.
