Chainflip loses bitcoin and ether in a two-chain vault theft.

Chainflip Labs says the 21 November 2026 theft took about 123 bitcoin as well as ether and dollar tokens. [1] The cross-chain swap service remains unavailable as the company offers to let the attacker keep a share if they return the rest. [2] [1]

At 07:01 UTC on 21 November 2026, a Bitcoin transaction sent 122.86 BTC to a single address. [3] At 07:10 UTC on 21 November 2026, one Ethereum transaction sent 520.22 ether, 3.25 million USDC and 758,141 USDT from Chainflip’s Ethereum vault to a different address. [4] Bitquery valued that Ethereum outflow at $4.86 million, of which USDC accounted for about two-thirds. [5]

Chainflip holds funds in vaults so users can swap assets across blockchains. [5] Its validators normally sign Ethereum payouts together, and Bitquery found that the vault accepted the signature on the drain. [5] The payment fell outside Chainflip’s usual numbering sequence, the only such exception among nearly 300,000 signatures Bitquery examined. [5]

Bitquery recorded a change of signing key at 08:53 UTC on 21 November 2026. [5] Chainflip Labs says it identified and addressed the way the key was exposed. [1] Its public notice gives no technical account of how that happened. [1]

The Bitcoin address had recorded no outgoing transfers by 10:30 UTC on 22 November 2026. [6] A message confirmed on 21 November 2026 pointed the recipient to Chainflip Labs’ offer. [7] The company says the recipient can keep 10% of the proceeds if roughly 110.58 BTC and 2,618.1 ETH arrive by 18:38 UTC on 23 November 2026. [1]

Chainflip Labs offered a plan to reimburse affected liquidity providers after a separate loss of about 736,000 USDT from its Tron vault on 12 September 2026. [8] The November recovery offer gives no terms for compensating users. [1]

Hong Kong proposes licences for four more crypto services.

The government published a bill on 20 November 2026 covering businesses that deal in, advise on, manage or hold virtual assets for clients. [9] Providers would need a licence or registration with the Securities and Futures Commission. [9] Banks offering those services would also need commission registration. [9] The government scheduled the first legislative reading for 2 December 2026. [9]

news

Russia’s rules for organised crypto trading take effect.

The Bank of Russia’s exchange ordinance takes effect on 22 November 2026, after a ten-day interval set by the text. [10] It adds reporting requirements for crypto contracts, including trade counts and total volume. [11] Under a separate ordinance, the central bank lists bitcoin, ether and USDT as available to non-qualified investors. [12] The central bank says those investors face a 300,000-ruble annual purchase limit per intermediary. [12]

news

Coins.ph ends transfers involving self-custody wallets.

In a notice updated on 21 November 2026, Coins.ph said it accepts external transfers only to and from accounts at regulated exchanges. [13] The policy took effect on 19 November 2026, and the exchange says it adopted it to comply with Philippine transfer reporting rules. [13] The exchange asks for the counterparty’s details on every external transfer, regardless of size. [13]

Ethereum developers discuss a January start for Glamsterdam.

The Ethereum Foundation’s Rahul Kothari told core developers on 19 November 2026 that mid-January 2027 was the earliest mainnet date he would suggest under the existing design. [14] Ethlabs says changes to what Ethereum charges for certain operations affect contracts built around fixed costs. [15] Developers pencilled in the week of 14 December 2026 for a Hoodi testnet upgrade, pending a formal decision. [14]

Solidity fixes a compiler bug that can corrupt stored data.

The Solidity team released version 0.8.39 on 19 November 2026. [16] The fix adds a runtime check for some copies of nested data, increasing their execution cost. [16] The team says it found no deployed contract performing an affected copy in the Sourcify database of verified contracts. [16]

Uniswap’s preliminary vote backs fees on five more chains.

The 21 November 2026 Snapshot close recorded roughly 27.9 million units of voting power in favour, with zero against or abstaining. [17] The proposal extends a fee policy that Hayden Adams, Ken Ng and Devin Walsh pitched as a way to pay for burning UNI. [18] Uniswap Labs says the expansion still needs an on-chain vote after a separate vote on the software that sets the fees. [19]

A DeFi coalition challenges the reach of Illinois’ digital asset tax.

The DeFi Education Fund said on 16 November 2026 that it and 17 industry organisations and businesses had submitted a proposed brief supporting a challenge to Illinois’ digital asset tax. [20] The coalition argues the law can reach developers and infrastructure providers without control over users’ transactions. [21] Its filing asks the court to find the law unenforceable. [21]
