PITCH C: Who owns bitcoin's gains in Britain's largest seizure

ARGUMENT: The fight over 61,000 seized bitcoin is no longer about returning stolen money. It is about who keeps the price gain. On 12 October 2026 the High Court ruled that Chinese law governs the victims' property claims [https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/kb/2026/2509].

FACTS: The judgment's agreed facts say victims paid about RMB 40.2bn (about £4.1bn) and RMB 34.1bn (about £3.4bn) was paid back, though not to every victim. Decrypt valued the coins at about $5bn on 13 October 2026 [https://decrypt.co/380818/uk-moves-closer-to-keeping-5b-bitcoin-haul-after-high-court-ruling].

INFERENCE: By my arithmetic from those figures, about £0.7bn of principal went unreturned. The coins are worth several times that. If claims are limited to principal, most of the surplus stays with the UK state.

UNCERTAIN: The content of Chinese law is still unproven. Fieldfisher, counsel for the claimants, is appealing, according to Financial Times reporting cited by Decrypt. Bitcoin.com also reports the ruling [https://news.bitcoin.com/regulation-and-legal/fraud-victims-hit-legal-wall-britain-5b-bitcoin-battle/].

UNANSWERED: Will the Treasury say what it plans to do with any surplus?

WHY NOW: The ruling is 19 days old and the appeal is pending. Most coverage stops at the headline.

READER LEARNS: How a choice-of-law ruling under the Proceeds of Crime Act section 281 [https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/29/section/281] may decide where billions go. Background comes from Caixin's report of the 11 November 2025 sentencing [https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-11-12/chinese-mastermind-of-uks-largest-bitcoin-laundering-plot-jailed-for-nearly-12-years-102381827.html].

CRITERIA: Consequence: billions at stake. Freshness: October 2026. Sourceability: two primary sources. Not covered to death: no prior edition led with it.
