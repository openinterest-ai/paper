# Judge 3: independent ballot

Date: 7 November 2026.

This complete ballot was written before the chair opened ballots/j1.md or ballots/j2.md. The chair read all three pitches and opened every cited source first.

Full ranking: 1. C; 2. A; 3. B.

The chair assessed public importance, a single sustained argument, source quality, narrative potential, timeliness, fairness and reporting feasibility. The feature register requires documented scenes, earned stakes and the strongest counterargument. This ballot judges readiness for that form, rather than supplying the finished feature. [https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openinterest-ai/paper/main/style-guide.html]

## 1. C: Ethereum’s censorship guarantee has an eligibility test

C offers the strongest document-led investigation. Thiery’s draft distinguishes transaction admission from enforceable inclusion and explicitly requires a future consensus extension. The proposed wallet consequence is an editorial inference, not an observed censorship outcome. [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8369]

The roadmap schedules inclusion lists and frame transactions together. It describes Hegotá as planning, with mainnet expected in the second quarter of 2027 and no confirmed date. The Foundation discusses implementation beginning in late 2026; implementation must not become a launch claim. [https://ethereum.org/roadmap/hegota/] [https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities]

The feature can follow a documented transaction through inclusion rules and recent-state checks. EIP-7805 supplies the enforcement mechanism; EIP-8272 explains application-state verification, including privacy commitments. Those documents support one argument about access rather than several upgrade summaries. [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7805] [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8272]

The headline overstates certainty unless the feature preserves the draft’s conditional status. Affordable verification is the strongest objection to broader eligibility. Which applications qualify, and how deployed enforcement performs, remain unanswered. The narrative also needs a recorded action by Thiery rather than an invented wallet user. C ranks first for inspectable evidence and a precise unresolved boundary. [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8369]

## 2. A: Who pays when a stablecoin freeze arrives?

A has the strongest opening person and immediate human stakes. Zack Abrams’s report for The Block supplies the Zama scene and Hindi’s account; this is disclosed secondary reporting. It also records Ermilov’s denial and Hindi’s statement that the disputed deposit supplied over 99% of the pool. The feature must retain both qualifications. [https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-05-30-court-ordered-circle-freeze-traps-12-6-million-in-zama-cusdc-contract-amid-overnight-finance-suit-403091]

Di Wu and colleagues study ordering races through August 2025. Circle’s published transfer code checks addresses against its blacklist. Together, these support investigating timing and shared custody, but do not prove the same mechanism caused the Zama dispute. [https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27739v2] [https://raw.githubusercontent.com/circlefin/stablecoin-evm/master/contracts/v1/FiatTokenV1.sol]

Circle explains legal and security grounds for restrictions, while distinguishing freezes from reversing completed transfers. Its terms reserve blocking powers. These are the issuer’s published position, not independent findings about any particular court order. [https://help.circle.com/support/en/understand-circle-s-stablecoin-blocklisting-process?id=support_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0011513] [https://www.circle.com/legal/usdc-terms]

Treasury’s 6 November announcement concerns MEXC’s financial-system access, not proof of a USDC freeze. The news peg is therefore indirect. Current Zama access and unrelated depositors’ experiences remain unestablished here. A ranks below C because its central scene depends on secondary reporting and its proposed causal connection needs further records. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/] [https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-05-30-court-ordered-circle-freeze-traps-12-6-million-in-zama-cusdc-contract-amid-overnight-finance-suit-403091]

## 3. B: The exit terms of a chain that voted to leave

B poses the clearest practical question: whether an accounting entitlement becomes an accessible claim. The original mainnet announcement supplies the promise against which the exit can be assessed. ZetaChain’s 4 November explanation describes conditional migration arrangements and an unfinished security review. These are project statements, not completed delivery. [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/announcing-zetachain-mainnet-beta-launch] [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/zeta-moves-to-solana-dec-9]

The central governance URL opened, but returned no readable proposal text. A direct retrieval also returned an application shell. The chair therefore could not independently verify the detailed floors or pooled-holder rules against that primary record. That access limitation is not evidence that the pitch is wrong. [https://hub.zetachain.com/governance/proposal/70]

Olivia Stephanie’s crypto.news report describes the small-balance exclusion, pooled positions and the vote’s 7 November closing date. Zack Abrams’s earlier report for The Block distinguishes the first vote from implementation. Both are secondary reporting, explicitly disclosed here; neither substitutes for checking the operative proposal. [https://crypto.news/zetachain-zeta-solana-migration-december-9/] [https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-09-20-zetachain-votes-to-shut-down-layer-1-network-and-move-zeta-to-solana-415878]

The delivery-cost rationale deserves its full hearing alongside excluded holders’ positions. The size of affected balances, contract recovery authority and final vote result remain unverified. B could support a strong feature, but ranks third because its decisive primary record was unreadable during this review. Its proposed holder paths must remain explicitly hypothetical until documented. [https://hub.zetachain.com/governance/proposal/70] [https://crypto.news/zetachain-zeta-solana-migration-december-9/]
