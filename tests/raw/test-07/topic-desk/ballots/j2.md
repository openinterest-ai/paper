# Ballot j2: Topic Desk, feature for 14-15 November 2026

Judge j2 checked all sources on 7 November 2026, between about 08:45 and 09:00 UTC. The question was which pitch should become next weekend's feature. The criteria were consequence, freshness, sourceability and under-covered value.

## Ranking

### First choice: Pitch C, "Ethereum's censorship guarantee has an eligibility test" (pitches/c.md)

Every checked claim matched its source, so Pitch C clears the first-place rule, and all five sources are open primary documents. Consequence is the widest of the three: EIP-8369 models which Ethereum transactions could compel inclusion, and it leaves blob transactions and ordinary privacy-contract storage outside [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8369]. Freshness is moderate, with mainnet expected in Q2 2027 and no confirmed date [https://ethereum.org/roadmap/hegota/], while one search found no dedicated reporting on the eligibility boundary.

### Second choice: Pitch B, "The exit terms of a chain that voted to leave" (pitches/b.md)

Freshness is the best of the three: the vote closes on 7 November 2026 at 16:12 UTC, and the halt is expected around 9 December 2026 [https://zetachain-api.polkachu.com/cosmos/gov/v1/proposals/70]. Sourceability is strong, but the pitch says ZetaChain gave a delivery-cost reason for its claim floors, and no opened text contains that reason, so the first-place rule bars it. Consequence is narrower, one token's holders against all of Ethereum, and Olivia Stephanie of crypto.news has already reported the small-balance rule [https://crypto.news/zetachain-zeta-solana-migration-december-9/].

### Third choice: Pitch A, "Who pays when a stablecoin freeze arrives?" (pitches/a.md)

Consequence is real because Circle reserves blocking power across USDC, but the pitch treats Zama's access as unanswered although Rand Hindi's 2 June 2026 post says the court lifted the freeze [https://www.zama.org/post/the-freeze-on-zamas-cusdc-contract-has-been-lifted-and-all-systems-are-back-to-normal]. The Wu paper finds no USDC race-window episodes and says most evasion moves earlier, which narrows the ordering half of the argument for USDC [https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27739v2]. Freshness rests on a Treasury release that never names USDC or Circle [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/], and the freeze itself is five months old.

## Facts, inferences and uncertainty

**Facts from the record**

- At 08:54 UTC on 7 November 2026, Proposal 70's live tally showed about 313.6 million ZETA for and 420.5 ZETA against [https://zetachain-api.polkachu.com/cosmos/gov/v1/proposals/70/tally]. The yes votes equal about 83% of the 376.7 million bonded ZETA [https://zetachain-api.polkachu.com/cosmos/staking/v1beta1/pool].
- The wrapped ZETA contract 0x5F0b1a82749cb4E2278EC87F8BF6B618dC71a8bf reported a supply of about 6.36 million through a public RPC call [https://zetachain-evm.blockpi.network/v1/rpc/public]. That is about 0.3% of the 2.1 billion maximum supply stated in Proposal 70.
- The EF Protocol cluster itself names EIP-8369 as the document defining FOCIL eligibility [https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities]. Pitch C does not use this support.

**This judge's inferences**

- Pitch C's thesis that censorship resistance will depend on wallet design is an inference, as the pitch itself says. It is drawn from a draft, and its constants remain "pending benchmarks".
- The wrapped ZETA figure shows that Pitch B's unquantified contract balances can be partly measured from chain data. It covers one contract, not all pools, and sub-10-ZETA balances remain unmeasured.
- Pitch A's question "who pays" has a weaker Zama answer than the pitch implies, because access returned within days. A feature would need another harmed party or a different opening.
- Pitch B would likely stay behind Pitch C on consequence even if its cost sentence were verified. Its freshness would make that a closer call.

**Uncertainty**

- The Proposal 70 hub page could not be rendered because a headless browser failed to install. The on-chain summary points to that page as "Full text", so the page could conceivably hold the cost sentence. The hub's code uses the same governance REST route family, which makes that less likely but does not exclude it.
- Both The Block articles blocked direct requests. The May article's wording reached this judge only through a fetch tool's extraction, so its quotes need checking against the page before publication.
- Hindi's statement that the court found the freeze "unwarranted" is Zama's account. The court docket was not opened.
- Under-covered judgments rest on one standard search per topic, not a coverage audit. Results were mostly seen as titles and search-tool summaries, and only the pages cited above were opened.
- The style guide shows its banned-word list as a sample plus "14 more". The unseen words could not be checked.

**Unanswered**

- Did Proposal 70 pass? The vote was open when this ballot was written.
- Who can authorize recovery of ZETA minted as the contract "remainder", and what happens to unclaimed vault balances? The on-chain text does not say.
- Which deployed wallets and privacy tools would fit Profile 2? EIP-8369 sets no final slot count, so none can yet be classified for enforcement.
- What was the Overnight Finance suit's later course, and did any Zama depositor report a loss? Neither was established here.
