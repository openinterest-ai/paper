# Topic desk ballot: Judge 1

**First choice: Pitch C, “Ethereum’s censorship guarantee has an eligibility test.”**

This ballot ranks consequence, freshness, sourceability and the prospect of an under-covered feature. Facts, inferences and unanswered questions are labeled below; a ranking is not a prediction of an outcome.

## Uncertainty

The cited documents set out drafts, proposals and conditional plans, not completed outcomes [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8369] [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/zeta-moves-to-solana-dec-9]. The unanswered passages name the records needed to resolve each reporting gap.

## 1. Pitch C — Ethereum’s censorship guarantee has an eligibility test

**Fact.** Draft EIP-8369 limits enforced inclusion to two transaction profiles, while Ethereum’s Hegotá roadmap schedules FOCIL and frame transactions but calls further scope unsettled [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8369] [https://ethereum.org/roadmap/hegota/].

**Inference.** The eligibility boundary is a consequential, timely and under-covered way to test a promised property against the types of wallet and privacy transaction it can cover; every cited record is a directly open primary specification or Ethereum publication [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7805] [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8272] [https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities].

**Unanswered.** The documents do not establish deployed performance or which applications will qualify, so the feature must present the effect as a proposed constraint rather than evidence of existing censorship.

## 2. Pitch B — The exit terms of a chain that voted to leave

**Fact.** ZetaChain’s 4 November post says that, if the vote passes, its L1 would halt around 9 December 2026, mint ZETA on Solana one-for-one and require at least 10 ZETA for an individual portal claim [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/zeta-moves-to-solana-dec-9].

**Inference.** The imminent vote and threshold give this pitch stakes, but sourceability blocks first place: the post calls it “Proposal 2” yet links an unreadable `/proposal/70` body [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/zeta-moves-to-solana-dec-9] [https://hub.zetachain.com/governance/proposal/70].

**Unanswered.** The proposed $5 bridged-asset floor, pooled-holder exit rule, block number and recovery route cannot be verified from that unreadable primary proposal; crypto.news, a secondary source, reports the 10-ZETA rule but cannot close that primary-record gap [https://crypto.news/zetachain-zeta-solana-migration-december-9/].

## 3. Pitch A — Who pays when a stablecoin freeze arrives?

**Fact.** The Block’s Zack Abrams, a secondary source, reported that a court-directed blacklist locked $12.6 million in Zama’s cUSDC contract and quoted Rand Hindi saying more than 99% related to the disputed deposit [https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-05-30-court-ordered-circle-freeze-traps-12-6-million-in-zama-cusdc-contract-amid-overnight-finance-suit-403091].

**Inference.** Pooled-custody collateral harm is consequential and under-covered, but sourceability is weak: the May event rests on secondary reporting and the MEXC action is not a USDC freeze [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/].

**Unanswered.** The supplied sources do not establish present access to the pool, the number of unrelated depositors or their losses; those questions need a court record, chain analysis or accounts from affected holders before publication.
