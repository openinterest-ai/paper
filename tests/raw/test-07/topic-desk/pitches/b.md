The exit terms of a chain that voted to leave

Argument: ZetaChain’s proposed shutdown exposes a gap between one-for-one token accounting and a holder’s ability to claim. Its 2024 mainnet announcement described a chain connecting Bitcoin, Ethereum and BNB Chain, with ZETA and other assets moving between networks [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/announcing-zetachain-mainnet-beta-launch].

Proposal 70 would halt that chain at block 20,755,000, expected around 9 December 2026, and move native ZETA to Solana. It sets a 10-ZETA minimum for individual claims, tells pooled holders to exit before the snapshot, and sets a separate $5 floor for bridged-asset claims. ZetaChain says delivery would cost more than balances below those floors [https://hub.zetachain.com/governance/proposal/70].

Why now: The second ballot closes 7 November 2026. Zack Abrams reported for The Block that September’s first vote approved the direction but left the mechanics open [https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-09-20-zetachain-votes-to-shut-down-layer-1-network-and-move-zeta-to-solana-415878].

ZetaChain’s 4 November explanation promises ZETA claims without user-paid fees and says Hacken’s security review remains in progress [https://www.zetachain.com/blog/zeta-moves-to-solana-dec-9]. Olivia Stephanie’s crypto.news report covers the shutdown and the small-balance rule [https://crypto.news/zetachain-zeta-solana-migration-december-9/]. The proposal does not quantify balances below its floors or held in contracts [https://hub.zetachain.com/governance/proposal/70].

The feature would reconstruct claim paths for a small holder, a pool depositor and a bridged-asset holder. It would separate the published claim rules from the promised snapshot and audit, then ask who can authorize recovery from contracts after the chain stops. The argument concerns access to a claim, not a proven loss.
