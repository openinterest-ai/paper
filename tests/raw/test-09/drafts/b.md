LEAD: Chainalysis says customer credentials were exposed in a cyberattack.

An attack on the firm used to trace crypto flows has exposed account data across its customer base. Chainalysis says it has no evidence that investigation records or attribution data were accessed or changed. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/]

Chainalysis’s November 6, 2026 update revised its October 30 account of an intrusion into the company’s systems. On November 2, the company says it discovered that names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users had been exposed to an attacker. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/]

The distinction between exposed access data and untouched investigation files matters here. Chainalysis sells tools used by exchanges, businesses and public agencies to examine transactions. [https://www.chainalysis.com/company/] The company says it has found no evidence that case notes, graphs, queries or transaction results were accessed, and no indication that the attacker manipulated its attribution data. Those are the company’s findings during an ongoing investigation, not an independent audit. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/]

The company took several products offline. On November 4, it also removed KYT, address screening and its login page after detecting abnormal activity, and said it was notifying customers whose KYT accounts appeared to have been accessed without authorization. Chainalysis planned to begin restoring KYT and address screening on November 5, while Reactor and Data Solutions remained offline as of its November 6 update. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/]

Chainalysis says it will require password resets and API-key rotation where appropriate. Its update calls the incident an “on-path attack,” and says it contained and remediated the attack vector that exposed a subset of customer passwords. It has not published a final account of how the attacker entered or whether any exposed credential was used against another service. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/]

The breach is separate from the October 29, 2026 theft at Binance, a connection Chainalysis explicitly rejects. Both incidents nevertheless put the security of companies behind crypto surveillance and custody under scrutiny, while the public record still lacks a completed forensic report for either event. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/security-incident-october-30/] [https://www.binance.com/en/blog/community/3229153443759566763]

NEWS

[NEWS] The FBI attributed the Binance theft to North Korea.

The FBI said on October 31, 2026, that North Korean actors stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The bureau calls the activity TraderTraitor and says stolen assets were being converted primarily into Ether. Binance’s November 3 update put the impact at about $6.34 billion and said about $2.85 billion had been frozen or intercepted; the company said 98% of affected assets had been replenished by that date. [https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/north-korea-responsible-for-6-billion-binance-attack/layout_view] [https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/384328127385979]

[NEWS] Mercado Bitcoin lost funds across 17 blockchains.

Chainalysis says the Brazilian exchange reported a November 2, 2026 security incident involving about $49 million in crypto assets. The analytics firm says the attacker moved assets across 17 blockchains, and that its work with Mercado Bitcoin, law enforcement and industry partners helped freeze nearly $10 million. These figures come from Chainalysis, which says it assisted the exchange; no independent total is established in the cited account. [https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/chainalysis-response-mercado-bitcoin/]

[NEWS] GOPAX suspended trading after receiving a hacking threat.

South Korean newspaper EToday reported on November 6, 2026, that GOPAX suspended crypto trading, deposits and withdrawals after receiving an email claiming the exchange had been hacked. EToday attributed the precautionary move to an exchange official and reported that the company had referred the matter to authorities. The cited report does not establish that GOPAX was breached or that customer assets were lost. [https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2645230]

TECH

[TECH] Ethereum developers paused Glamsterdam testing over contract risks.

The Ethereum Foundation said on November 6, 2026, that testing found projects relying on assumptions that EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 would break. The proposals change gas charges for state creation and access. Core developers delayed forking the Hoodi testnet until they resolve the issues, and the Foundation asked contract developers and auditors to check eight affected invariants. [https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/11/06/glamsterdam-8037-8038]

[TECH] Nethermind measured denial-of-service risks in frame validation.

Three Nethermind researchers reported November 6, 2026, that Ethereum’s proposed EIP-8141 validation budget of 100,000 gas is too low for some privacy proofs and post-quantum signatures. Their benchmark found that raising the limit to 300,000 could be viable with a client safeguard that pauses transaction validation while importing a block. In their tests, that safeguard shifted pressure to the mempool, which admitted only 12% to 27% of honest frame transactions during a simulated flood; these are Nethermind’s results, not a network-wide test. [https://ethresear.ch/t/how-large-can-eip-8141s-validation-budget-be-a-mempool-dos-benchmark/26224]

CULTURE

[CULTURE] Algorand is closing the forum and pausing xGov grants.

The Algorand Foundation said on November 6, 2026, that it would close its forum and end its Ecosystem Advisory Council that day, then pause xGov grants indefinitely from December 6. The Foundation said the forum had mainly hosted xGov discussion and directed builders to Discord, with a read-only archive planned. It also shelved Project King Safety, including previously discussed changes to the ALGO supply cap, MEV capture and minimum transaction fee, while saying staking rewards remain committed through January 2028. [https://algorand.co/blog/algorand-community-update]

[CULTURE] Optimism proposed a formal route out of shared upgrade control.

An Optimism governance proposal posted November 5, 2026, would let a Standard Rollup chain’s governor request transfer of upgrade authority from the Security Council to an address of its choice. The proposed process requires Foundation confirmation and a public notice at least 30 days before the transfer; completion would end the chain’s Standard Rollup status. The proposal remains a ratification vote, changes Charter language only and executes no on-chain transaction by itself. [https://gov.optimism.io/t/proposal-to-introduce-upgrade-authority-transfer-to-the-standard-rollup-charter/10950]
