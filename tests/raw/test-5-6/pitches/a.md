Ethereum's past needs a maintenance budget

The feature will argue that cheaper Ethereum nodes transfer preservation work to people whose continued service needs scrutiny. This is an inference from draft EIP-4444, which identifies availability risks and leaves preservation incentives outside its scope. [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4444]

Why now: Eth Docker's August 20, 2026 release shortened rolling retention for Reth, Besu and Erigon configurations from one year to approximately five months. This makes retrieval beyond the retention window an operational reporting subject. [https://github.com/ethstaker/eth-docker/releases/tag/v26.8.1]

The benefit is real: Matt Garnett's Ethereum Foundation announcement estimates that removing pre-Merge blocks saves each node 300–500 GB. It distinguishes historical records from current account state, which remains available. [https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/07/08/partial-history-exp]

Readers will learn how evidence survives after an ordinary node discards it. Geth's Download Era documentation describes retrieving historical bodies and receipts and checking downloaded files against checksums. [https://geth.ethereum.org/docs/fundamentals/downloadera] The Ethereum History Providers directory lists mirrors and a torrent for pre-Merge mainnet records. [https://eth-clients.github.io/history-endpoints/]

The reporting will follow a historical transaction through those retrieval routes, separating authenticity from availability. Provider independence, sustained funding and retrieval reliability remain unanswered; a directory cannot establish them. These are five checked, open primary sources; no secondary reporting supports this pitch.

The fresh angle is the maintenance obligation behind retrospective scrutiny, with consequences for researchers reconstructing transactions. The rehearsal's prior leads concern Hoodi, stateless computation, SEACAST and signatureless authorization; none leads with history preservation.
