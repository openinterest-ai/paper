Ethereum's past needs a maintenance budget.

The feature will argue that cheaper nodes shift preservation costs onto archivists, making access to old evidence a separate public obligation. EIP-4444 leaves preservation incentives and availability monitoring outside its scope. [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4444]

Why now: the 28 September 2026 EIP-4444 amendment more than halved its proposed history-retention window. The proposal remains Draft. [https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/commit/f951d789d5e2f65ce8ac8994f7e0f4f4affdec43] [https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4444]

The opening follows Matt Garnett's 8 July 2025 announcement: removing pre-Merge blocks could save 300–500 GB, helping nodes fit on 2 TB disks. His defense supplies the counterargument: historical data remains verifiable when a supplier provides it. [https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/07/08/partial-history-exp]

Geth's History Pruning documentation describes deleting old transaction records while retaining current balances and contract data. [https://geth.ethereum.org/docs/fundamentals/historypruning] Readers learn why checking new transactions differs from reconstructing old events.

The eth-clients history-provider registry lists archive mirrors. [https://eth-clients.github.io/history-endpoints/] Portal's History Network specification describes distributed retrieval, a possible defense against dependence on a few providers. [https://github.com/ethereum/portal-network-specs/blob/master/history/history-network.md]

The distinctive angle is who sustains public access after storage becomes optional. The argument concerns an availability risk; it does not establish lost records. Mirror independence and durable funding remain unanswered.

The rehearsal archive has no prior lead on history preservation. [https://openinterest-ai.github.io/paper/rehearsal.html] All cited pages were checked; technical claims use primary sources only. No secondary reporting is used.
