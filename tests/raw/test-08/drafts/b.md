LEAD:
FinCEN orders American financial institutions to stop MEXC transfers. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf]

The November 6, 2026 order covers cryptocurrency as well as conventional funds, with enforcement tied to Federal Register publication. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] MEXC says it remains operational and has begun an independent review of its controls. [https://www.mexc.com/announcements/article/statement-regarding-fincen-s-november-6-order-17827791539626]

American financial institutions face a prohibition on sending funds to MEXC Global or receiving funds from it. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] The restriction reaches the exchange's overseas subsidiaries and successor businesses, extending beyond the MEXC name. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] FinCEN issued the order under legislation targeting Russian money laundering. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/]

FinCEN attributes $16.7 billion in suspected illicit activity to the exchange between May 2021 and May 2026. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] Treasury says nearly $1.5 billion involved Russian sanctions evasion, including $232 million transferred directly with sanctioned entities. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/] Those figures describe alleged transaction flows across five years, rather than a customer-account shortfall. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf]

Treasury says MEXC continued dealing with Garantex and its successor Grinex in June 2026, despite sanctions. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/] It alleges that deficient customer controls allowed business from jurisdictions the exchange's own policies excluded. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/]

The prohibition starts 30 days after Federal Register publication; the posted order leaves that calendar date blank. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] Its terms contain no automatic expiry, although FinCEN retains authority to grant exemptions. [https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-11/MEXC-Section-9714.pdf] Existing sanctions and suspicious-activity reporting obligations remain in place. [https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0685/]

MEXC says its legal and compliance teams are reviewing the order and that customer assets remain secure. [https://www.mexc.com/announcements/article/statement-regarding-fincen-s-november-6-order-17827791539626] It promises engagement with regulators and an independent examination of anti-money-laundering and sanctions controls. [https://www.mexc.com/announcements/article/statement-regarding-fincen-s-november-6-order-17827791539626] Its statement supplies neither a rebuttal of FinCEN's transaction totals nor a timetable for the review. [https://www.mexc.com/announcements/article/statement-regarding-fincen-s-november-6-order-17827791539626]

The Central Bank of Nigeria issued licensing rules for non-security virtual assets in a November 5, 2026 circular. [https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/REGULATIONS%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20NON-SECURITY%20VIRTUAL%20ASSETS%20IN%20NIGERIA.pdf] They prohibit issuing or trading algorithmic stablecoins and fiat-referenced tokens backed mainly by other crypto assets in Nigeria. [https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/REGULATIONS%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20NON-SECURITY%20VIRTUAL%20ASSETS%20IN%20NIGERIA.pdf] Stablecoin issuers cannot pay holders interest merely for holding their tokens, and providers cannot use customer assets for their own purposes. [https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/REGULATIONS%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20NON-SECURITY%20VIRTUAL%20ASSETS%20IN%20NIGERIA.pdf]
