LEAD

The FBI says North Korea took $6.3 billion from Binance, and about half of it can no longer be frozen.

Binance lost roughly $6.3 billion from its hot wallets on 29 October 2026, the largest crypto theft on record. The FBI named North Korea's TraderTraitor group on 31 October, and Binance reopened withdrawals in phases on 1 November.

The theft took about 20 minutes. TRM Labs counts roughly $6.36 billion leaving Binance wallets across 10 blockchains and attributes the operation to TraderTraitor with high confidence [https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korea-steals-usd-6-36-billion-from-binance-in-the-largest-hack-in-history]. CoinDesk, citing TRM, lists 18,270 BTC (about $1.47 billion) and 1.875 billion USDT among the assets [https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/11/01/binance-opens-crypto-withdrawals-in-phases-starting-with-bitcoin-following-usd6-3-billion-hack].

The FBI, with the Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said the thieves were converting assets mainly into Ethereum. It listed 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address for operators to block [https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/north-korea-responsible-for-6-billion-binance-attack]. The Block reports that the alert did not disclose its evidence.

Recovery is limited by asset type. Elliptic counts $6.43 billion stolen and $2.85 billion, about 44%, frozen or seized, 94% of it stablecoins. About $3.25 billion, including 18,327 BTC, sits in assets no issuer can freeze [https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-01-north-korean-hackers-launder-242-million-of-binance-loot-via-tron-as-fbi-presses-thorchain-analyst-432257].

Binance says it has replaced about 95% of affected assets, up from 90% a day earlier, using $6.34 billion of company funds [https://crypto.news/binance-restores-usdt-usdc-eth-bnb-withdrawals/]. Co-CEO Richard Teng cites a compromised laptop [http://www.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/article-913440]. How one machine reached systems holding billions is unexplained. A spokesperson told CoinDesk: "Binance will bear the financial impact of the stolen funds." Bitcoin withdrawals reopened first on 1 November, with other networks gated on security checks. A full zero-knowledge proof of reserves is expected by 3 November. Tether froze 378.7 million USDT on Tron, but the unfreezable Bitcoin is the open question.

NEWS

[NEWS] Bridges and swap services processed $194 million of Binance loot, and THORChain halted trading.

SomaXBT counts $242 million laundered on Tron in two days, of which about $194 million left Tron through bridges and swaps. Relay handled $48 million, THORChain $44 million, Chainflip $32.1 million and Symbiosis $16 million [https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-11-01-north-korean-hackers-launder-242-million-of-binance-loot-via-tron-as-fbi-presses-thorchain-analyst-432257]. Symbiosis said its screening "has gaps." THORChain paused after an Ethereum reorg caused a double refund of a 10 ETH deposit, and as of 1:20 p.m. ET on 1 November trading was halted on 10 of 13 connected chains. Co-founder Chad Barraford said node operators have "zero information" beyond what is public and did not say whether they will block the FBI's addresses.

[NEWS] Polymarket paused trading on 29 October after a compromised vendor script drained about $2.5 million.

Polymarket moved to cancel-only mode at 2:05 p.m. ET and resumed trading at 3:05 p.m. It said "One of our third party providers was compromised, and we have referred this to relevant law enforcement" [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/polymarket-halts-trading-after-crypto-draining-script-breach-25m-traced/]. The script was served from DebugBear's monitoring service and hijacked logged-in sessions. BleepingComputer traced about 28.6 million POL, roughly $2.57 million, to three wallets, while Hudson Rock reported the wallet held about $2.19 million. Polymarket has not said how many users were affected, and it said affected users would be made whole.

[NEWS] A domain hijack redirected 15 DeFi front ends to a wallet drainer on 31 October.

Blockaid first found malicious pages on 12 sites, all registered with Name.com, serving the Step Drainer kit. Name.com later said 15 client domains were redirected without authorization [https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2026-10-31-hackers-hijack-a-dozen-defi-websites-all-registered-with-name-com-and-steal-about-138000-432251]. Reported losses are about $138,000, a floor that excludes Solana and Aptos, and Bitquery estimated $202,386 across 32 wallets [https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/SOL/6ae6be2cbbcb5200074562b4]. The smart contracts were not breached. Neither Name.com nor Vercel, which hosted 10 of the sites, has said how the attackers gained control of domain settings.

TECH

[TECH] A new scheme would identify encrypted-mempool committee members who sell decryption shares early.

Aditi Partap and Arantxa Zapico posted on Ethereum Research on 5 October 2026 a traceable threshold encryption design for committee-based encrypted mempools [https://ethresear.ch/t/accountability-for-encrypted-mempools/26123]. A tracer queries a leaking coalition's decoder box with perturbed shares and interpolates a polynomial whose roots name the corrupt parties. The authors call it "a first step" and not deployment-ready. It needs a trusted setup authority that could itself forge shares, and it relies on factoring assumptions that are not post-quantum secure.

[TECH] Ethereum researcher prices the sender's "free option" in encrypted mempools at about 1 basis point.

The Ethereum Research post by arianaraghi, dated 22 October 2026 and revised 28 October, defines the free option as the value a sender gains by controlling when a transaction is revealed [https://ethresear.ch/t/reveal-optionality-in-encrypted-mempools-how-big-is-the-sender-s-free-option-and-a-post-quantum-way-to-remove-it/26169]. With ETH implied volatility near 51% and an 8-second window, the exogenous option is about 1 basis point, and a fee of about 6 basis points would cap exercise at 1%. The proposed Blind Simultaneous Release scheme uses hash-based commitments, and the paper reports 116 Lean 4 theorems. The hardware speedup figures are an explicit assumption, and the author asks for real data on sequential-hash ASICs.

CULTURE

[CULTURE] Compound's multisig cancelled a pending proposal, and tokenholders moved to strip its veto.

On 22 October 2026 the five-of-nine Community Multisig cancelled Proposal 618, which would have moved stewardship of a $5 million emergency fund to the multisig, before tokenholders could vote [https://btcwires.com/crypto-regulations-law/compound-governance-proposal-guardian-multisig-cancellation-power/]. Delegates then filed "End Community Multisig Cancellation Power," which would end the guardian role at 00:00 UTC on 1 November. Critics say two cancelling signers received advisory compensation from the Compound Foundation. This rests on one secondary outlet dated 26 October, and the vote outcome is unverified.

[CULTURE] The White House response to the Binance theft is a shrug, and security writers are not buying Binance's account.

Speaking at Mar-a-Lago on 31 October, President Trump said "We're looking into it. I'm not worried," and that he would discuss the matter with North Korea if its role is established, as reported by crypto.news citing SBS News [https://crypto.news/binance-restores-usdt-usdc-eth-bnb-withdrawals/]. The FBI had attributed the theft to North Korea the same day. The newsletter This Week in Security questions how a single compromised engineer laptop could reach systems controlling billions [https://this.weekinsecurity.com/this-week-in-security-november-1-2026-edition/]. The two positions leave open who will press Binance for a full incident report.
